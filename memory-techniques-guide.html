The Memory Techniques Guide

Forgetting is normal. The good news is that a few well-tested techniques make memories much stronger, and most take less time than re-reading.

1How forgetting works

Without review, much of what we learn fades within days. Each time you successfully recall something, the memory becomes stronger and fades more slowly. That is why when and how you review matter more than how long you study.

2Retrieval practice

Retrieval practice means pulling information out of memory rather than putting it back in. It works better than re-reading because the effort of recalling strengthens the memory.

  • Cover your notes and write down everything you remember.
  • Answer practice questions before looking at the answers.
  • Use flashcards, saying the answer before turning the card.
  • Teach the topic to someone else, or to an empty room.

3Spaced repetition

First1 day after learning

The exact gaps matter less than the pattern: each gap longer than the last. The Spaced Repetition Scheduler works out the dates for you.

4Interleaving

Instead of practising ten problems of one type, mix types together. It feels slower, but it trains you to recognise which method to use, which is exactly what an exam asks of you.

5Memory aids

  • Mnemonics: first letters or a short rhyme for lists.
  • Visual links: a quick sketch or diagram for processes.
  • Chunking: grouping long numbers or lists into smaller sets.
  • Elaboration: asking “why?” and “how does this connect?”

6Sleep, breaks and attention

Memory consolidates during sleep, so a good night before an exam is worth more than an extra late hour of study. Short breaks keep attention fresh, and putting your phone in another room removes the biggest single distraction.
